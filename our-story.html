Our Story

Hedges come and go. Summers run dry. Somebody spreads ash, somebody else forgets to. Every bit of it settles into the ground beneath you, one thin layer at a time. We made Fallow so that anyone with a garden could read it.

001

It started in a shed at Aldermoor, with eleven years of cores nobody had asked for.

Background

Stratum Lab is a small team that grew out of the Aldermoor Institute of Soil Science. It uses robotic handling and laser sampling to read the record stored inside each core. Undisturbed soil gains roughly one centimeter a season, and each one is a chapter. We lay those chapters out side by side, date them as closely as the ground allows, and add a note on what to do about each.

A hunch you can check

Perhaps the beans always fail in one corner. Perhaps the new compost seems better, but only on sunny days. A core turns that hunch into something you can point at.

Why order matters

Stir a sample and you lose the story. Keep it whole and each layer stays beside its neighbours, so a change shows up as a line you can date rather than a number you have to guess about.

Jobs, not jargon

The last page is always a short list. What to add, what to leave alone, and what to sow first. Nothing that needs a digger, and nothing that can’t wait for a dry Saturday.

003

Dr Adeyo began with hedges, and with one stubborn question: what has this ground lived through?

Overview

For eleven years Dr Adeyo drove a borrowed van between hedges, orchards, and fields that had been given up on, taking one core from each. Laid side by side on a bench, they looked like bookshelves. Droughts repeated. Floods lined up. Owners came and went. That idea became the Aldermoor Layer Method in 2020, and it is still how every core here is read, yours included.
